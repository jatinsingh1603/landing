The Root Cause

The problem wasn't India's cyber defence.
It was the vendors.

In Operation Sindoor — and every breach before it — the attack vector was the same. Not the government firewall. Not the internal network. The third-party vendor.

Compromised vendor credentials — vendors with access to government systems had weak or reused passwords. Attackers walked right in.

Unmonitored vendor API endpoints — third-party integrations left open without security audits. Data exfiltrated silently.

Vendor email chain exploitation — phishing through trusted vendor email addresses bypassed security filters entirely.

No vendor vetting standard — vendors onboarded on self-declaration forms. No compliance checks. No security audits.

Incident — AIIMS Delhi
2022 · India

India's Premier Hospital.
Paralysed for Two Weeks.

AIIMS Delhi — the country's most important government hospital — was hit by ransomware that crippled all digital systems for over 15 days. Patient records, appointments, billing — everything went offline. Millions of patient records were affected.

The attack exploited gaps in external systems and security controls — including outdated practices, weak network segmentation, and insufficient access controls.
